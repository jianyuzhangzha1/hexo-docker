### The Second Time in Kanto

This was my second time in Japan, and this year we stayed in Tokyo for 9 days. Our guesthouse was near Azuma-bashi Bridge, in a small tatami room on the second floor. The staircase was narrow, the corner was tight, and the street was quiet enough that we did not see many tourists dragging suitcases around. It felt less like staying in a hotel and more like temporarily borrowing a small piece of local life.

Everyone had a different wish for this trip, so Tokyo became a family schedule made of anime, museums, shopping streets, temples, and food.

# Bakery

There was a bakery near Azuma-bashi subway station. When the queue was long, more than twenty people would be waiting outside. My daughter liked it very much, so we almost visited it every day for breakfast.

At first, I was not used to this rhythm: we bought bread, walked back to the guesthouse to eat, and then took the tram out for the day. But after a few mornings, this became one of the small rituals of the trip.

![Bakery](/images/2025/tokyo/bread.png)

The bakery also has a branch in Ginza. If a bakery can open a shop in such a prime location, it must have something special. No wonder so many people were willing to queue. Surprisingly, it also accepted WeChat Pay.

# Meiji Shrine and Demon Slayer

We went to Meiji Shrine, which was one of my daughter's wishes. After the noise of the city, the shrine area felt calm and green, and the long walk under the trees made Tokyo suddenly feel slower.

![Meiji Shrine](/images/2025/tokyo/2_mingzhi_tempale.jpg)

We also took our daughter to watch the new Demon Slayer film. Watching Japanese anime in a Japanese cinema felt like completing a small side quest of the trip. The audience was quiet and focused, and the whole experience felt very different from watching anime at home.

![Demon Slayer theater](/images/2025/tokyo/1_theartor.jpg)

During the trip, I also saw campaign posters of the Japanese Communist Party and even passed by their headquarters. It was a small but interesting reminder that Tokyo is not only shrines, shopping, and anime; it is also a real city with its own politics and daily public life.

![Japanese Communist Party poster](/images/2025/tokyo/3_japanese_community_party.jpg)

# Ikebukuro and Akihabara

This year we unlocked Ikebukuro. For my daughter, it was another important stop in the anime world. Compared with Akihabara, Ikebukuro felt more like a shopping area mixed with pop culture. There were many goods stores, character shops, and young people moving between buildings with very clear goals.

![Goods day with my daughter](/images/2025/tokyo/5_goods_day_my_daughter.JPG)

Akihabara was a continuation of last year's two-dimensional journey. I still feel that Akihabara is not just a place for buying things; it is more like a visible archive of Japanese pop culture. Every floor has a different layer of desire, memory, and marketing. It is tiring, but it is also very alive.

![Akihabara summer festival](/images/2025/tokyo/summer_ji.JPG)

# Ghibli Museum

The Ghibli Museum was one of the most anticipated stops. Tickets were very hard to get, and I did not manage to buy them at the original price. In the end, I bought three expensive tickets on Ctrip. A Chinese person gave us the tickets near the subway station and even helped us get on the bus, which made the whole process feel a little like a travel mission.

Unfortunately, it rained that day. Even so, the museum was still worth visiting. I saw Miyazaki's studio, manuscripts, hand-drawn illustrations, color separation, animation production techniques, and photos of the prototype of the bathhouse in *Spirited Away*. I felt again that Japanese animation is built on a very realistic eye: the creators are good at discovering beauty from ordinary real scenes and then refining it into something dreamlike.

The small cafe on the first floor was also a surprise. The snacks were delicious, maybe even better than expected on a rainy museum day.

# Tokyo National Museum

Tokyo National Museum was my own wish. I went to see exhibitions about swords, samurai, and tea ceremony.

![Sword exhibition](/images/2025/tokyo/7_sword_museum.JPG)

The museum was wonderful for me, but unfortunately my mom and child were not interested at all. I only stayed for half a day, which was a pity. I could probably spend much longer there alone, especially in the rooms with swords and old armor. Those objects carry a very quiet kind of power.

# Asahi TV and Characters

We also went to Asahi TV and happened to catch Doraemon's birthday. Doraemon is a classic, of course, but I still prefer Crayon Shin-chan. Maybe Shin-chan feels closer to ordinary family life: noisy, funny, sometimes rude, but warm in the end.

# Food

There was an eel rice restaurant that was quite expensive, with one dish costing more than 4,000 yen. The queue was long, and the restaurant was decorated with many old photos showing the Asakusa Festival in earlier times. It had a strong feeling of the past.

The restaurant was run by two elderly ladies and only opened for lunch. At first, I felt the price was a little high, but later I realized that eel rice is considered a relatively high-end dish in Japan. It is probably enough to eat it two or three times a year as a special meal.
